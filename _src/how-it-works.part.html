Step one

Install it where the data should live

Regnum ships as a single Windows installer. It sets up the application and its database on the machine you choose — a reception PC, the back-office desktop, or a small server if you have one. Nothing is provisioned in the cloud, and nothing phones home to work.

  • Runs on ordinary hardware — no dedicated server required
  • Other staff reach it from their own browsers over your local network
  • Works with the internet down; a clinic's day should not depend on a link
Step two

Tell it about your practice

A first-run wizard asks the handful of questions that shape everything else: how many sites, what you dispense, who works here and what each of them should be able to see. It takes about ten minutes and can be changed later without a migration.

  • Create sites or storerooms and name them the way staff already do
  • Add users and assign one of four roles
  • Set your currency, financial year and stock categories
Step three

Import the file you already keep

Upload your stock list and, if you have it, your consumption history. Regnum reads the columns, shows you its interpretation, and asks you to confirm the mapping once. Rows it cannot parse are listed individually with the reason — you fix them in place rather than starting over.

Accepts

CSV, XLSX, and exports from most dispensing systems

Reports

Rows imported, rows skipped, duplicates merged

Keeps

Every upload versioned, so any import can be rolled back

Step four

Let the models find your baseline

With history loaded, Regnum builds a consumption profile per item — the ordinary week, the seasonal shape, the items that move in bursts. From that it derives reorder points, expiry risk and the first forecasts. If your history is short, it says so and starts conservative.

  • Twelve months of history gives a strong baseline; three months gives a usable one
  • Forecasts refresh as new movement is recorded — no monthly rebuild
  • You can override any suggestion, and the override is remembered
Step five

The week, as it actually runs

Most days, Regnum is a glance. The dashboard shows whether anything needs attention; if it does, the alert links straight to the item and the action. Once a week someone runs the refill suggestions and turns them into an order. Once a month the finance export goes to the bookkeeper.
